# New Hire Training and Certification Programme

> Fictional reference document — Meridian Fulfillment Co.

**Document ID:** TRN-NEW-001
**Owner:** Client Operations Manager, with Facility Managers
**Last reviewed:** 2026-07-02
**Applies to:** All new permanent hires in warehouse operations and client operations
**Related:** VEN-POL-002 governs temporary worker induction, which is a separate and
shorter programme

---

## Purpose

Defines what a new Meridian employee must know, how it is taught, how it is assessed, and
what they may not do until assessed.

**This programme was written in 2026 and did not previously exist in consolidated form.**
Training was delivered facility by facility from materials each Facility Manager maintained
independently. This mirrors the problem the company profile identifies with onboarding
documentation — *"each Onboarding Specialist has evolved their own checklist variant"* — and
the same drift had occurred in training.

Two incidents contributed directly to this document:

- **INC-2026-0104**, in which one client's goods shipped to another client's customers. The
  review found that no training material anywhere in the business explained *why* client
  segregation matters, only that it is required.
- **The 2024 peak**, in which eleven temporary workers received abbreviated induction and
  accounted for 31% of pick errors in weeks 47–52 while representing 12% of hours.

## 1. Programme structure

| Track | Roles | Duration | Certification |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Core** | All operations hires | 2 days | Required before floor access |
| **Warehouse — Receiving** | Receiving clerks | +3 days | Required before solo work |
| **Warehouse — Putaway** | Putaway operators | +2 days | Required before solo work |
| **Warehouse — Pick/Pack** | Pickers, packers | +3 days | Required before solo work |
| **Warehouse — Returns** | Returns processors | +2 days | Required before solo work |
| **Inventory Control** | Cycle counters | +3 days | Required; Core + 90 days tenure prerequisite |
| **Client Support** | Support Specialists | +5 days | Required before queue access |
| **Billing** | Billing Analysts | +5 days | Required before invoice work |
| **Onboarding** | Onboarding Specialists | +4 days | Required before owning an account |

Certification is **role-specific and does not transfer.** A certified picker moving to
receiving completes the receiving track.

## 2. Core track — all operations hires

### Module C1 — Who we are and who our clients are (2 hours)

- Meridian Fulfillment Co., founded 2016, headquartered Richmond VA
- Three facilities: Richmond VA, Columbus OH, Reno NV — 410,000 sq ft total
- 240 employees, 87 active clients, roughly 19,000 orders on a normal day
- **The three client tiers and why they differ:**

| Tier | Clients | Volume | Service model |
|---|---|---|---|
| Enterprise | 12 | 50,000+ orders/month | Dedicated account manager, custom SLAs, monthly business reviews |
| Growth | 31 | 5,000–50,000 orders/month | Shared account management, standard SLAs, quarterly reviews |
| Standard | 44 | Under 5,000 orders/month | Pooled support, standard SLAs, self-service portal |

**Assessed understanding:** an enterprise client is not "more important as a person." They
have purchased a different service level, and in some cases contractual remedies that
Growth and Standard clients do not have. The difference is commercial, not moral.

### Module C2 — Client inventory custody (2 hours)

**The most important module in the Core track.** Added 2026-04 following INC-2026-0104.

Content:

- **The inventory in this building is not ours.** Meridian owns almost nothing on the racks.
  Every unit belongs to one of 87 clients, and Meridian holds it in custody.
- **What custody means:** a legal and contractual obligation to keep each client's goods
  identifiable, intact, and separate from every other client's goods.
- **Why segregation matters, concretely:** taught using INC-2026-0104 as a case study. Six
  pallets placed one level below their directed location. Forty-three orders shipped a
  different client's product to end customers. 387 units gone, 108 never recovered,
  $11,850 in credits, two clients who nearly left.
- **What to do when you are not sure:** stop and ask. The cost of asking is a few minutes.
  The cost of guessing, in the case study, was twelve days and two client relationships.

The case study is taught **with the client names anonymized** but with the numbers intact.
Trainees consistently report the numbers are what makes it land.

**One deliberate teaching choice:** the module states plainly that the operator who made
the putaway error was not disciplined, and explains why. The system accepted the wrong
location without objection, the labels differed by one character, and the lighting was
below standard. Blaming the operator would have left all three conditions in place.

This matters because a workforce that believes errors are punished hides errors. The
custody module's practical goal is that someone who realizes they have misplaced product
says so immediately.

### Module C3 — Safety (4 hours)

- PPE requirements by zone
- Powered equipment awareness; pedestrian rules
- Aisle discipline — including the 42-inch mezzanine aisles at Reno
- Racking damage: what to look for, how to report, never work under damaged racking
- Emergency procedures, assembly points, incident reporting
- Injury reporting: **any injury affecting service escalates immediately to the Client
  Operations Manager** per POL-ESC-001, bypassing the normal path

### Module C4 — The WMS handheld (4 hours)

- Login, shift assignment, task acceptance
- Scanning technique; when a scan fails and what to do
- Task completion and confirmation
- Error states and what each means
- **Never share a login.** Every transaction is attributed. A shared login makes an error
  untraceable, which converts a fixable process problem into an unknowable one.

### Module C5 — When something goes wrong (3 hours)

- The four severity levels, in plain terms:

| Severity | Plain meaning | First response |
|---|---|---|
| **S1 — Critical** | A client's business is stopped, or something is broken for many clients at once | 30 minutes |
| **S2 — High** | Something significant is broken but there's a way around it, or one enterprise client is materially affected | 2 hours |
| **S3 — Medium** | Contained. One order, one dispute, one report | 1 business day |
| **S4 — Low** | A question. Nothing is broken | 2 business days |

- **You are not expected to classify severity.** You are expected to recognize when
  something might be S1 or S2 and tell a supervisor immediately.
- The six things that escalate immediately regardless of who you are, per POL-ESC-001:
  suspected theft or internal loss; any data or security incident; a client threatening to
  terminate; any injury or safety incident affecting service; any regulatory or compliance
  matter; media or social media attention.
- **Never promise a client anything.** Not a delivery date, not a credit, not a root cause.
  Per POL-ESC-001, service credits may never be committed below Level 3, and root cause is
  never stated before investigation concludes.

### Module C6 — Quality and the cost of error (1 hour)

Taught with real numbers, because abstractions do not land:

| Error | Downstream cost |
|---|---|
| One misput pallet | Up to 12 days undetected, 43 wrong shipments, $11,850 credits (INC-2026-0104) |
| One unread SKU mismatch warning | The same incident — the warning fired 43 times |
| One missing photo at receiving | Carrier claim denied — over 80% denial rate without photos |
| One skipped blind recount | A caught discrepancy becomes an inventory variance found months later |
| One late carrier claim | Claim forfeited entirely — the 48-hour window is the carrier's, not ours |

## 3. Warehouse — Putaway track (+2 days)

**Certification prerequisite for all putaway work. At Reno, additional requirements apply.**

### Module P1 — Directed putaway (3 hours)

- How the WMS assigns a home location
- Reading the task: LPN, SKU, quantity, directed location, client, special handling
- **Verbalize the location before travelling.** Taught as technique, with the evidence:
  operators who verbalized misread at roughly a third the rate of those who did not
  (SOP-PUT-007 observation study).
- Scan **location first, then LPN**. The reason: the system should know where you are
  standing before it knows what you are holding.

### Module P2 — Handling a mismatch (3 hours)

The three options and when each applies:

| Option | Use when |
|---|---|
| **Go to the correct location** | Default. Almost always. |
| **Request a re-direct** | The directed location is genuinely unusable |
| **Supervisor override** | System state is wrong in a way re-direct cannot fix |

Re-direct reason codes: `OCCUPIED`, `NO-FIT`, `BLOCKED`, `DAMAGED`.

**Taught explicitly: re-direct is not an exception path. It is the normal path when the
direction is wrong.** Re-directs are expected and are not counted against the operator.
A re-direct rate near zero is a warning sign, not a good result — the target band is 2–6%.

**Supervisor override is monitored.** Q2 2026 data showed 14 of 43 post-enforcement PUT-01
events at Reno involved an override that should not have been granted. Trainees are taught
that override exists for system-state problems, not for saving time.

### Module P3 — Placement (2 hours)

In priority order:

1. **Label facing the aisle.** Non-negotiable — cycle counters cannot count what they cannot
   read.
2. Heaviest at the bottom for multi-tier locations.
3. No overhang. In the mezzanine this is a safety issue, not tidiness.
4. Lot-tracked: verify lot on LPN matches physical cartons. Mixed lots defeat FEFO.
5. Serialized: never mix serial ranges in a location.

**For clients with lot requirements**, the rules can be stricter than the general case.
Northwind Provisions requires no lot commingling in a pick face at all, enforced at
putaway. Client-specific rules appear on the task's special-handling flag.

### Module P4 — Reno supplement (4 hours, Reno only)

Required for any operator working Reno putaway.

- **The ground/mezzanine label problem.** Ground reads `14-A-03-2`; mezzanine reads
  `14-A-03-2M` (old) or `M14-A-03-2` (new). Both schemes are live in the building during
  relabeling.
- **How to check the zone:** press and hold the location field on the handheld; it states
  `GROUND` or `MEZZ` explicitly.
- **Mezzanine rules:** verify zone before travelling; no solo mezzanine putaway after 19:00
  on second shift; report any mezzanine `NO-FIT` verbally to the supervisor same shift;
  never stage product in mezzanine aisles.
- **Why:** aisles 14–22 are 18% of pick faces and produced 61% of misputs. The relabeling
  is 57% complete and the lighting remediation is unfunded.

**Temporary workers may not perform mezzanine putaway at Reno at all**, per VEN-POL-002.

## 4. Warehouse — Receiving track (+3 days)

### Module R1 — Standard receiving (4 hours)

Covered under SOP-REC-001.

### Module R2 — Discrepancies (8 hours)

The full SOP-REC-004 procedure. The most assessment-heavy module in the warehouse tracks.

Trigger conditions, then the eight steps. Emphasis on:

- **Step 1: quarantine before recording.** Once stock enters the sellable pool, isolating
  the variance requires a full cycle count. Quarantine first is faster in every case.
- **Step 2: photos are mandatory** for any damage or seal-integrity issue.
- **Step 3: blind recount by a second person.** Roughly a third of reported discrepancies
  resolve at recount. Do not tell the second counter the first count.
- **Step 5 thresholds:** under 2% and under 50 units — record, notify the account manager by
  end of day. At or above 2%, or 50+ units — hold for written client confirmation.
  **Overage of any size — hold entirely.** Never absorb an unexplained overage; it is
  frequently another client's stock.
- **Step 6: file the carrier claim within 48 hours.** Claims outside this window are
  generally denied.

**Immediate escalation regardless of step:** variance above $10,000, any enterprise-tier
client, seal broken with quantity shortage (treat as potential theft), any suspicion of
internal loss.

**Taught as a specific trap:** the enterprise-tier escalation applies to *every* enterprise
discrepancy, including small ones that would be routine for a Standard client.

### Module R3 — LTL and carrier documentation (4 hours)

- Inspect before signing. Always.
- **Cardinal LTL: damage not noted on the delivery receipt at delivery is effectively
  unrecoverable.** Note damage on the BOL even when the extent is unclear — "subject to
  inspection" preserves the claim, a clean signature forfeits it.
- Required claim documentation, per CAR-POL-001.

## 5. Client Support track (+5 days)

### Module S1 — The escalation matrix (8 hours)

Full POL-ESC-001. Severity definitions, response and resolution targets, escalation path,
communication rules.

**The enterprise uplift is taught explicitly and assessed:** enterprise clients receive one
severity level of priority handling. An S3 from an enterprise client is worked at S2 targets
— 2-hour first response, updates every 4 hours.

Taught using INC-2026-0038 as a case study, where the uplift was not applied, an enterprise
integration failure sat in the pooled queue for 5h16m, and a 45-hour misclassification let
1,847 orders drop.

**The lesson drawn is not "check the tier."** It is *quantify before you characterize.* The
first ticket said two orders were missing. Nobody asked whether those were the only two or
merely the two the client noticed. The count took under four minutes when finally run.

### Module S2 — What you may and may not say (4 hours)

| May say | May never say |
|---|---|
| Acknowledgement | A root cause before investigation concludes |
| Investigation status | A resolution timeframe not confirmed and scheduled |
| Factual updates | Anything about a service credit |
| "I don't know yet, and here is when I will" | A delivery date |

- **Service credits: never below Level 3.** If a client asks directly whether they will be
  compensated, the correct answer is that the Account Manager will address it. This is hard
  under pressure and is roleplayed in assessment.
- **S1 and S2 client contact is Senior Account Manager or above — never a Support
  Specialist.** Taught with INC-2025-0417, where two specialists responded directly to
  enterprise tickets during the WMS outage. Both responses were accurate and neither caused
  harm; the failure was the absence of a routing rule, since fixed.
- **Never blame the client**, even where the cause was on their side. Also never blame a
  vendor or carrier — the client contracted with Meridian.

### Module S3 — Billing questions (8 hours)

Support Specialists do not resolve disputes, but they take the first contact.

**The five most common disputes**, per POL-FIN-003, in frequency order:

1. **Storage billed higher than expected.** Storage bills on **peak occupancy** in the
   period, not average, not month-end. A client who received a large inbound mid-month and
   sold through by month-end still pays on the peak. Correct per contract, and the single
   most disputed line.
2. **Peak surcharge on the January invoice.** The surcharge applies to the **service date**,
   not invoice date. Week 52 orders bill in January carrying the surcharge.
3. **Dimensional weight.** Per carrier tariff when DIM exceeds actual weight.
4. **Manual order entry fees.** Usually indicates an integration failure the client has not
   noticed — investigate root cause, do not just explain the fee.
5. **Long-term storage surcharge.** Applies at 181 days at the SKU-lot level, not account
   level.

**Rates differ by tier.** FIN-RATE-2026-01 is the Standard card at $28.00 per pallet
position per month. Growth and Enterprise clients have negotiated schedules — Lumen Bath Co.
pays $26.25, Northwind Provisions $24.50. **Never quote the Standard card to a Growth or
Enterprise client.** Check the account's schedule.

**Taught as the single highest-risk error in the role**, because quoting a wrong rate to a
client creates a commercial problem that is difficult to walk back.

### Module S4 — Intake and triage (8 hours)

- All disputes enter through Zendesk tagged `billing-dispute`, regardless of how raised. If
  raised verbally or in a meeting, the Account Manager creates the ticket.
- Required at intake: invoice number and date, line items disputed, amount disputed,
  client's stated reason, whether payment is being withheld.
- **Acknowledge within 1 business day, always, even before investigation.**
- Severity re-check at 24h and 48h on any open incident — added after INC-2026-0038.

## 6. Assessment and certification

### Method

| Component | Weight | Pass mark |
|---|---|---|
| Written assessment | 40% | 80% |
| Practical observation | 40% | Satisfactory on all critical items |
| Scenario roleplay (client-facing roles only) | 20% | Satisfactory |

**Critical items fail the whole assessment regardless of overall score.** These are:

- Placing product without scanning the location
- Sharing a WMS login
- Signing an LTL delivery receipt without inspection
- Committing a service credit, delivery date, or root cause to a client
- Absorbing an overage into sellable inventory
- Skipping a blind recount
- Working under damaged racking

### Re-assessment

One re-assessment permitted after additional coaching. A second failure moves the employee
to a different track or role.

### Recertification

| Track | Interval |
|---|---|
| Core — safety module only | Annual |
| Warehouse tracks | Every 2 years, or after any critical-item incident |
| Reno putaway supplement | Annual, until accuracy holds at target for 2 consecutive quarters |
| Client Support | Annual |
| Billing | Annual, or on any rate card revision |

**Recertification after a critical-item incident is not disciplinary** and is stated as such
in the material. It exists because the most common cause of a critical-item error is a
process condition the employee could not control, and recertification is the point at which
that condition gets surfaced.

## 7. Programme metrics

Reported quarterly to the VP Operations.

| Metric | Target | Q2 2026 actual |
|---|---|---|
| Core certification before floor access | 100% | 100% ✅ |
| Role certification before solo work | 100% | 97.4% ❌ |
| First-attempt pass rate, warehouse tracks | ≥ 85% | 88.1% ✅ |
| First-attempt pass rate, Client Support | ≥ 80% | 74.2% ❌ |
| Recertification completed on schedule | ≥ 95% | 91.6% ❌ |
| 90-day retention, certified hires | ≥ 85% | 82.3% ❌ |

**Role certification at 97.4%** — five employees performed solo work before certification.
All five at Reno, all during the April–May period when the facility was short-staffed and
the putaway investigation was consuming supervisor time. This is a control failure and is
recorded as one.

**Client Support first-attempt pass at 74.2%** is below target. Analysis of failures shows
the scenario roleplay is where candidates fail, specifically on declining to commit to a
credit when pressed. This may indicate the module needs more practice time rather than that
candidates are unsuitable.

**90-day retention at 82.3%** is dragged down by Reno, where permanent turnover runs 31%
annually against 19% at Richmond (VEN-POL-002). Reno's staffing instability is plausibly a
contributing factor to its accuracy problem — SOP-PUT-007 found operators under 90 days
tenure accounted for 29% of PUT-01 events, a disproportionate share though not the dominant
one.

## 8. Known limitations

1. **Training completion is tracked in a spreadsheet**, not a system. VEN-POL-002 flags the
   same weakness for temporary workers. A missing record cannot be distinguished from a
   missing certification.
2. **No assessment of retention over time.** Certification is a point-in-time test. Nothing
   measures whether an operator certified 18 months ago still follows the procedure.
3. **The Reno supplement is reactive.** It teaches operators to work around a labeling
   problem rather than the problem being fixed. It is the right response given the
   relabeling timeline and it should become unnecessary — Action 2 of SOP-PUT-007 is due
   2026-08-31.
4. **Client Support training assumes normal staffing.** It does not cover the peak triage
   model in SOP-PEAK-001, under which S3 and S4 targets extend and seasonal staff handle S4
   only from scripted responses. Seasonal support staff receive a separate abbreviated
   induction that is not covered by this programme and is not assessed to the same standard.
5. **No module covers the onboarding checklist's commercial walkthrough**, added to
   SOP-ONB-001 in the 2026-07 revision after ONB-CASE-2026-058. The Onboarding track needs
   updating and has not been.

Limitation 5 is the most immediately actionable and is assigned to the Client Operations
Manager for the Q3 revision.
